Nutrition for Mental Health

Feed your mind with the right nutrients

What you eat affects how you feel. Research shows that a diet rich in certain nutrients can help reduce symptoms of depression, anxiety, and other mental health conditions. Discover foods that can boost your mood and cognitive function.

Foods for Mental Wellness

Salmon

Rich in omega-3 fatty acids

Salmon

Omega-3 • Vitamin D • Protein

Salmon is an excellent source of omega-3 fatty acids, which are essential for brain health. Research has shown that omega-3s can help reduce symptoms of depression and anxiety. Salmon is also high in vitamin D, which has been linked to improved mood and reduced risk of depression.

Reduces inflammation Improves mood Supports brain function

Blueberries

Antioxidant powerhouse

Blueberries

Antioxidants • Vitamin C • Fiber

Blueberries are packed with antioxidants, which help reduce inflammation and oxidative stress in the brain. These berries are particularly rich in flavonoids, which have been shown to improve memory and cognitive function. Regular consumption of blueberries has been linked to a reduced risk of age-related mental decline.

Protects brain cells Enhances memory Reduces inflammation

Dark Chocolate

Mood-boosting treat

Dark Chocolate

Flavonoids • Magnesium • Theobromine

Dark chocolate contains several compounds that can enhance mood, including phenylethylamine, which triggers the release of endorphins and can promote feelings of pleasure. It's also rich in flavonoids that improve blood flow to the brain, enhancing cognitive function. The higher the cocoa content (70% or more), the greater the benefits.

Boosts mood Reduces stress Improves focus

Spinach

Leafy greens rich in nutrients

Spinach

Folic Acid • Antioxidants • Iron

Spinach and other leafy greens provide your brain with solid amounts of folic acid, which has been shown to be a great deterrent to depression. It also helps fight off insomnia, which is heavily linked to mental impairments and can help reduce dementia in older adults.

Fights depression Improves sleep Cognitive protection

Yogurt

Probiotics for gut-brain health

Yogurt

Probiotics • Protein • Calcium

Yogurt and other products containing active cultures are excellent sources of probiotics. Often associated with digestive health, probiotics have been shown to play a role in reducing stress and anxiety. Yogurt can also provide you with potassium and magnesium, which helps oxygen reach the brain, further improving its ability to function.

Reduces stress Improves gut health Brain oxygenation

Nuts

Healthy fats and protein

Nuts

Omega-3 • Antioxidants • Vitamin E

Like salmon, nuts are an excellent source of omega-3 fatty acids, helping to fight depression. Cashews, for example, help provide oxygen to the brain with a dose of magnesium. Almonds contain a compound called phenylalanine, which is shown to help the brain produce dopamine and other neurotransmitters that boost your mood. Phenylalanine has also been linked to a reduction in the symptoms of Parkinson's Disease.

Mood enhancement Brain oxygenation Neurotransmitter support

Olive Oil

Healthy fats and antioxidants

Olive Oil

Polyphenols • Monounsaturated Fats • Antioxidants

Pure, extra virgin olive oil has been quite popular as of late as a part of healthy Mediterranean-style diets. This type of oil contains polyphenols, which help to remove the effects of proteins linked to Alzheimer's Disease. It can also help improve learning and memory. Be careful when shopping for olive oil, however. Many brands liberally cut their product with vegetable or seed oils, significantly reducing its brain health benefits. Research brands online to find brands tested to ensure they contain pure olive oil.

Cognitive protection Improves memory Anti-inflammatory

Tomatoes

Rich in lycopene

Tomatoes

Lycopene • Antioxidants • Vitamin C

The source of a tomato's red hue, lycopene is classified as an all-around beneficial phytonutrient. One of the many health boosts it provides is in the fight against brain disease. It's been shown to delay the onset and progression of Alzheimer's Disease, fighting off cell damage. In addition, lycopene has been shown to help with memory, attention, logic and concentration.

Cognitive protection Improves memory Enhances concentration

Chicken

Lean protein source

Chicken

Lean Protein • Tryptophan • B Vitamins

Chicken, like turkey, is a delicious lean-protein choice containing the amino acid tryptophan. Though it's often associated with post-Thanksgiving naps, this substance doesn't actually knock you out as urban legends go, but it does help your body produce serotonin — which is vital in helping regulate your mood. Chicken also contains the B vitamins niacin and B-6. Niacin is particularly important for converting food to energy and metabolizing fat, protein and carbohydrates.

Mood regulation Energy production Protein for brain health
